## Introduction

[Latvia]({{ 'latvias-strangest-stories-between-myth/' | relative_url }})’s haunted places are rarely just stories about ghosts. They are also stories about ruined castles, vanished social worlds, war, imprisonment and the memories attached to old buildings. Across the country, manor houses, medieval castles and military sites have collected legends of spectral women, unexplained sounds and restless spirits. These accounts are not evidence that ghosts exist, but they show how communities use supernatural stories to preserve the emotional history of places. Latvia’s most famous haunted locations work as cultural records: the building is real, the history is often documented, and the ghost story becomes an added layer of meaning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://folklore.lv/?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: folklore.lv">[folklore.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">folklore.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Latvian folklore</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 31, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2024</span></span></span>

Castles such as Ēdole Castle and Gārsene Castle have become associated with ghostly figures and romantic legends, while Karosta Prison has developed a modern haunting reputation built around its harsh history as a military prison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/en/sight/edole-castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TravelĒdole Castle &#124; latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ēdole Castle &#124; latvia.travel...</span></span></span> Together, these places reveal a recurring pattern in Latvian ghost traditions: the “haunting” often reflects human suffering, social change and the power of memory as much as any claim of paranormal activity.

## Castles, manors and the white lady motif

Historic buildings provide the perfect setting for Latvian ghost traditions because they already contain traces of dramatic human stories. Medieval fortresses, Baltic-German manor houses and military buildings have witnessed conflict, political upheaval and personal tragedies. Over time, memories of these events can become attached to a particular room, corridor or landscape, producing legends of apparitions and mysterious happenings.

One recurring figure in Latvian haunted-place traditions is the ghostly woman, often described as a white-clad lady. This motif is not unique to Latvia and appears widely across European folklore, but in Latvia it often becomes linked to specific buildings and local histories. The figure usually represents loss: a betrayed lover, a woman who died tragically, or someone whose story was never properly resolved. Rather than functioning simply as a frightening monster, the ghost becomes a reminder of an unhappy past.

Ēdole Castle is one of the country’s best-known examples. The castle dates back to the medieval period, was expanded and remodelled over centuries, and became associated with numerous legends. Tourism and heritage descriptions note that the site is known for ghost stories, including tales connected with mysterious marks and dramatic events said to have taken place inside the castle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/en/sight/edole-castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TravelĒdole Castle &#124; latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ēdole Castle &#124; latvia.travel...</span></span></span> These stories are difficult to verify as historical events, but their survival shows how old buildings can become containers for collective imagination.

Another example is Gārsene Castle, a 19th-century Neo-Gothic manor complex. The castle’s modern visitor experience includes the legend of the Blue Lady, a kind-hearted ghost associated with the building. Rather than presenting the story only as horror, local tourism has incorporated it into interpretation of the castle’s atmosphere and history, showing how ghost traditions can become part of cultural heritage and visitor engagement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/en/sight/garsene-castle" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel Gārsene Castle &#124; latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gārsene Castle &#124; latvia.travel...</span></span></span>

The importance of these legends is not that they prove supernatural activity. Their value lies in what they preserve. A story about a ghostly woman may keep alive memories of aristocratic estates, vanished families, wars or social changes long after the original historical circumstances have faded.

## Karosta Prison and modern hauntings

If castles provide Latvia’s traditional ghostly atmosphere, Karosta Prison represents the modern haunted place: a building whose real history is already disturbing enough to inspire legends. Built around 1900, the prison served military authorities and later became associated with several regimes that controlled Latvia during the twentieth century. Today it is open to visitors as a museum and unusual historical attraction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latvia.travel/en/sight/naval-port-prison" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latvia.travel">[Latvia Travel]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Travel The Naval Port Prison &#124; latvia.travel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Naval Port Prison &#124; latvia.travel...</span></span></span>

Stories connected with Karosta include footsteps, unexplained noises, doors moving and sightings of a female figure often called the White Lady. A popular version of the legend tells of a woman who entered the prison searching for her fiancé and died after discovering he had been executed. The story is preserved through local retellings rather than established historical records, but it has become one of the site’s defining legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.celotajs.lv/en/e/story/view/karostascietums/ff80808125afced80125b071ea950019" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: celotajs.lv">[CeloTAJs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">celotajs.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Celo TAJs The Karosta prison</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Karosta prison - StoryDecember 21, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 2009</span></span></span>

Karosta’s reputation has also been strengthened by paranormal tourism. The site promotes its unusual atmosphere and notes that the prison has been described by ghost-hunting enthusiasts as one of the world’s most haunted locations. Such claims belong to the history of the legend rather than scientific proof of ghosts. The documented reality is already compelling: the prison housed military personnel and political prisoners, and visitors can still see traces of former inmates in the building.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.karosta.lv/sakums-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: karosta.lv">[karosta.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">karosta.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Home...</span></span></span>

The Karosta example shows why haunted places are often created around sites of suffering. A prison does not need a supernatural explanation to feel unsettling. Isolation, historical violence, abandoned spaces and knowledge of past events can all shape how visitors interpret what they experience.

## Why haunted places preserve memory

Latvian ghost traditions are best understood as a meeting point between folklore and history. The country’s folklore collections contain vast numbers of legends, beliefs and traditional narratives, providing evidence of how communities recorded ideas about spirits, unusual events and the unseen world. The Archives of Latvian Folklore preserves millions of items, including manuscripts, recordings and other materials documenting traditional culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lfmi.lu.lv/en/archives-of-latvian-folklore" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lfmi.lu.lv">[lfmi.lu.lv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lfmi.lu.lv</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Archives of Latvian Folklore – LU LFMI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archives of Latvian Folklore – LU LFMI</span></span></span>

Haunted-place stories continue this older tradition in a modern setting. A castle corridor, an abandoned manor or a former prison becomes a stage where people negotiate questions about the past. Why did a family disappear? What happened during a war? Who suffered inside these walls? A ghost story often provides an emotional answer when historical records leave gaps.

There is also a practical reason these stories survive. Legends make heritage sites memorable. A visitor may forget architectural details or dates, but a tale of a mysterious figure walking through an old corridor is easier to remember and share. This does not make the story historically true, but it explains its cultural strength.

For Latvia, haunted places are therefore less about proving the existence of spirits and more about understanding how landscapes collect stories. The ghost in the castle, the footsteps in the prison and the mysterious figure in the manor all represent the same idea: old places continue to carry the voices people attach to them.
