## Introduction

Medjugorje is one of the world's most famous and most disputed modern religious vision sites. Since June 1981, six young people from the Herzegovinian village have said they experienced repeated appearances of the Virgin Mary, whom they call the "Queen of Peace". Millions of pilgrims have visited, reporting conversions, renewed faith and, in some cases, remarkable healings. At the same time, the Catholic Church has spent decades examining the claims without declaring that the visions themselves are supernatural.

That careful distinction is what makes Medjugorje especially significant in [Bosnia and Herzegovina]({{ 'bosnia-and-herzegovina/' | relative_url }})'s modern Fortean landscape. Unlike many legendary apparition stories, this is an ongoing phenomenon with living witnesses, extensive documentation, scientific scrutiny, theological debate and an unusually cautious official response. ## The 1981 vision claims

The story began on 24 June 1981, when six local teenagers reported seeing a luminous female figure on a hillside near Medjugorje. They identified her as the Virgin Mary after subsequent encounters. According to their testimony, she called for prayer, fasting, repentance and peace during a period of growing political tension in what was then socialist Yugoslavia.

Unlike many historical Marian apparitions, which involved only a handful of reported encounters, the Medjugorje seers claimed the experiences continued over months and, for some visionaries, have continued for decades. Thousands of alleged messages have been published, making Medjugorje unusual among Catholic apparition claims.

Supporters point to several recurring features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* consistent emphasis on prayer, reconciliation and peace;
* the apparent sincerity of the original witnesses;
* numerous personal testimonies of spiritual conversion;
* reports of unusual experiences during pilgrimages, including unexplained healings.

</div>

Critics, however, note that the sheer volume of messages, the long duration of the alleged apparitions and occasional inconsistencies in testimony distinguish Medjugorje from historically recognised apparition sites such as Lourdes or Fátima. They argue that prolonged visionary claims naturally become more difficult to evaluate and may reflect psychological, social or cultural influences rather than supernatural intervention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20240919_nota-esperienza-medjugorje_en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vatican.va">[Vatican]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vatican.va</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vatican“The Queen of Peace”: Note About the Spiritual Experience...19 Sept 2024 — “I have presented myself here as the Queen of Peace to...</span></span></span>

## Why millions still make the journey

Whatever the origin of the reported visions, Medjugorje has become one of the world's busiest Catholic pilgrimage destinations. Visitors often describe experiences that are deeply personal rather than overtly miraculous: renewed religious commitment, reconciliation with family members, recovery from addiction, or a rediscovered sense of purpose.

Claims of miraculous physical healing have also circulated since the early years. As at other pilgrimage centres, these reports vary greatly in quality. Some are supported by medical records submitted by pilgrims, while many remain anecdotal. The Church has never presented healing stories from Medjugorje as proof that the apparitions are genuine.

Researchers studying pilgrimage have suggested several overlapping explanations for the site's impact:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-stack" markdown="1">

* the emotional intensity of collective worship;
* expectation and suggestion;
* powerful religious symbolism;
* the psychological effects of retreat and reflection;
* genuine religious experience, as understood by believers.

</div>

These explanations are not necessarily mutually exclusive. A person may experience authentic spiritual renewal without that, by itself, proving that every reported apparition occurred exactly as described.

The village itself has also changed dramatically. Once a quiet farming community, it now supports churches, retreat houses, hotels, religious shops and an international pilgrimage economy. ## Why the Vatican remains cautious

The Catholic Church distinguishes between the value of a place of pilgrimage and the authenticity of the supernatural claims associated with it. Medjugorje illustrates that distinction more clearly than almost any other modern case.

For decades, local bishops, theologians and Vatican commissions reached differing conclusions about the reported apparitions. In 2010, Pope Benedict XVI established an international commission chaired by Cardinal Camillo Ruini to study the phenomenon in greater depth. Although aspects of its work later became publicly known, its recommendations were advisory rather than definitive.

Meanwhile, Pope Francis often praised the spiritual good experienced by pilgrims while expressing reservations about the idea of continuous scheduled messages from the Virgin Mary. ## What changed in 2024?

In September 2024 the Vatican's Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith issued the note *The Queen of Peace*, giving Medjugorje a *nihil obstat*—Latin for "nothing stands in the way". This represents an important pastoral decision but not a declaration that the apparitions are authentic.

The document makes several points simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Catholics may participate in pilgrimages and devotion connected with Medjugorje.
* Many positive spiritual fruits have been observed among pilgrims.
* The Church does **not** declare the alleged apparitions to be supernatural.
* Individual messages attributed to the visionaries should still be read with discernment.
* The emphasis should remain on Christ, prayer and the Gospel rather than fascination with extraordinary phenomena.

</div>

The note also reflects broader reforms introduced by the Vatican in 2024 for assessing alleged supernatural events. Under the new norms, formal recognition of supernatural origin is expected to be exceptionally rare. Instead, the Church may encourage healthy devotional practice even while leaving unresolved the question of whether extraordinary experiences genuinely came from heaven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20240919_nota-esperienza-medjugorje_en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vatican.va">[vatican.va]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vatican.va</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vatican“The Queen of Peace”: Note About the Spiritual Experience...19 Sept 2024 — “I have presented myself here as the Queen of Peace to...</span></span></span>

## Why Medjugorje matters as a Fortean case

From a Fortean perspective, Medjugorje occupies an unusual middle ground. It is neither dismissed outright as a hoax nor accepted by the Catholic Church as a proven miracle. Instead, it remains a living example of how extraordinary claims can acquire lasting cultural importance without receiving definitive supernatural endorsement.

Its continuing fascination comes from several overlapping factors:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* living eyewitnesses whose testimony can still be examined;
* decades of reported visionary experiences rather than a single historical episode;
* millions of pilgrims producing a vast body of personal testimony;
* extensive theological and scientific investigation;
* an official Church position that deliberately separates spiritual value from factual certainty.

</div>

For believers, Medjugorje demonstrates that profound religious experiences continue in the modern world. For sceptics, it illustrates how expectation, memory, social influence and devotion can sustain extraordinary narratives across generations. The Vatican's position leaves room for both serious faith and careful questioning, making Medjugorje one of the most distinctive unresolved religious phenomena in contemporary Europe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20240919_nota-esperienza-medjugorje_en.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vatican.va">[vatican.va]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vatican.va</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vatican“The Queen of Peace”: Note About the Spiritual Experience...19 Sept 2024 — “I have presented myself here as the Queen of Peace to...</span></span></span>
