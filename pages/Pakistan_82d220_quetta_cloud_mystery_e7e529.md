## Introduction

In October 2025, an unusual glowing shape appeared above Quetta’s mountains and quickly became a modern sky mystery. Images shared online showed a smooth, bright, saucer-like formation above the Koh-e-Murdar range, leading some viewers to suggest a UFO, missile launch, or secret aerial activity. The explanation was far more familiar: meteorologists identified the spectacle as a rare lenticular cloud, a natural formation created when moist, stable air flows over mountains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thecurrent.pk/fact-check-meteor-missile-or-cloud-truth-behind-mysterious-glow-in-quetta-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecurrent.pk">[The Current]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecurrent.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CurrentFact check: Meteor, missile or cloud? ## The images that spread online

The event unfolded in the early hours over Quetta, when residents noticed an unusual glowing formation above the eastern mountain area. According to reports from the [Pakistan]({{ 'pakistans-strangest-stories-between-fact/' | relative_url }}) Meteorological Department (PMD), the cloud appeared before sunrise, remained visible for roughly 20 minutes, and disappeared as daylight increased.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dawn.com/news/1951777/rare-lenticular-cloud-formation-over-quetta-leaves-netizens-puzzled-over-origins" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dawn.com">[Dawn]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dawn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dawn‘Rare lenticular cloud formation’ over Quetta leaves netizens puzzled over origins - Pakistan - DAWN.COM...</span></span></span>

Photographs and videos transformed a brief atmospheric event into a nationwide curiosity. The smooth, curved shape resembled the classic “flying saucer” design familiar from science fiction, while the unusual colours created by low-angle sunlight made the formation appear even more artificial. Social media posts quickly circulated with speculation about UFOs, missile tests and other unexplained possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thecurrent.pk/fact-check-meteor-missile-or-cloud-truth-behind-mysterious-glow-in-quetta-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecurrent.pk">[The Current]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecurrent.pk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The CurrentFact check: Meteor, missile or cloud? This pattern is common in modern sky mysteries: the original observation is often accurate, but the explanation attached to it changes as people attempt to classify something unfamiliar.

A viewer looking at the Quetta images might reasonably notice:

* A sharply defined, symmetrical shape rather than a typical fluffy cloud.

## Lenticular clouds and mountain weather

The key to the Quetta sighting is the science of lenticular clouds. These are lens-shaped clouds that form when air moves over mountains and creates standing waves in the atmosphere. If the air contains enough moisture, clouds can develop at the rising parts of these waves, producing smooth, layered shapes that can appear almost stationary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/clouds/clouds-form-mountains" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scied.ucar.edu">[Center for Science Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scied.ucar.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ucar.edu.</span></span></span>

Mountains around Quetta provide the necessary ingredients. When winds encounter mountain ranges, the air is forced upwards, where it cools and water vapour condenses into visible cloud. The cloud can appear fixed in one place because fresh moisture is continuously forming at one point while older cloud material evaporates elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scied.ucar.edu/image/lenticular-clouds" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scied.ucar.edu">[Center for Science Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scied.ucar.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Science Education Lenticular Clouds &#124; Center for Science Education</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Science Education Lenticular Clouds &#124; Center for Science Education</span></span></span>

This creates one of the most deceptive features of lenticular clouds: they can look like objects rather than weather.

Unlike ordinary clouds that drift visibly across the sky, lenticular formations often keep their shape for a period of time. Their smooth edges, stacked layers and hovering appearance have made them one of the most common natural explanations for historical “UFO” reports around mountainous areas. The UK Met Office notes that these clouds can resemble traditional flying-saucer imagery and are frequently mistaken for unusual aerial objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/clouds/unusual-cloud-formations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: weather.metoffice.gov.uk">[Met Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">weather.metoffice.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Met Office Unusual cloud formations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Met OfficeUnusual cloud formations - Met Office...</span></span></span>

The Quetta case fits this wider pattern. The event was unusual, but unusual does not necessarily mean unexplained. A rare atmospheric arrangement can create a scene that looks technological or impossible while still following ordinary physical laws.

## Why the sky became a mystery

The Quetta episode shows how visual misidentification works in the age of instant sharing. A person seeing an unfamiliar object in the sky does not begin with a complete scientific dataset. They see a shape, compare it with familiar ideas, and try to decide what category it belongs to.

For much of history, unusual skies produced rumours of omens, supernatural signs or strange celestial events. Today, the same human tendency appears through different explanations: unidentified aircraft, military experiments or spacecraft. The technology has changed, but the process of interpretation remains similar.

Several factors made the Quetta cloud especially likely to attract speculation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A familiar “spacecraft” shape:** Popular culture has strongly associated saucer-like forms with alien technology.
* **Limited visual information:** A photograph rarely shows scale, distance, wind conditions or movement accurately.
* **A dramatic setting:** A glowing object above mountain peaks naturally feels more mysterious than the same cloud over an ordinary landscape.
* **Rapid online sharing:** Social platforms reward striking images before explanations catch up.

</div>

Research into misinformation and online sharing patterns in Pakistan has highlighted how quickly uncertain claims can spread through digital networks, especially when content is visually striking or emotionally engaging.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09338" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Investigating Misinformation Dissemination on Social Media in Pakistan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Investigating Misinformation Dissemination on Social Media in PakistanJune 17, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2021</span></span></span>

The Quetta cloud was not a case of people inventing a mystery from nothing. They were responding to a genuinely unusual sight. The mistake was not seeing something strange; it was assuming the strange appearance required a strange cause.

## How social media reshapes mysteries

The Quetta cloud mystery belongs to a new generation of Fortean-style stories where cameras and smartphones play a central role. In earlier eras, unusual sightings travelled through newspapers, word of mouth and personal testimony. Today, a single image can reach millions of people before experts have time to identify what it shows.

This changes the life cycle of a mystery:

1. **A rare event appears.**

A natural phenomenon creates an unexpected visual scene.
2. **Images circulate.**

Viewers encounter the event without local weather information or scientific context.
3. **Interpretations compete.**

Ordinary explanations and extraordinary possibilities spread side by side.
4. **Specialists investigate.**

Meteorologists, scientists or knowledgeable observers identify the likely mechanism.

The Quetta case demonstrates why visual evidence needs interpretation. A photograph can prove that something was seen, but it does not automatically reveal what the thing was.

This distinction is central to many strange-sky reports around the world. A meteor may look like a falling object; a planet may appear to be a hovering light; a cloud may resemble a machine. The image records the appearance, not the cause.

## The lasting appeal of the Quetta sky mystery

The Quetta cloud remains interesting precisely because it sits between wonder and explanation. It was not a confirmed UFO encounter, nor was it simply an ordinary cloud that nobody noticed. It was a rare atmospheric event that briefly transformed the familiar sky into something that looked impossible.

For Pakistan’s record of strange reports, the event is valuable because it captures a modern form of mystery-making. Ancient landscapes, dramatic weather and digital communication combined to create a moment where nature appeared artificial.

The lesson of the Quetta sky is not that mysteries disappear when science provides an explanation. Instead, the explanation often reveals a different kind of wonder: the atmosphere itself can create shapes strange enough to imitate the inventions of science fiction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scied.ucar.edu/image/lenticular-clouds" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scied.ucar.edu">[Center for Science Education]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scied.ucar.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Science Education Lenticular Clouds &#124; Center for Science Education</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Science Education Lenticular Clouds &#124; Center for Science Education</span></span></span>
