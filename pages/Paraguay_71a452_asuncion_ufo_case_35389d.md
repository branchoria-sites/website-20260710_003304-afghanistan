## Introduction

The 1963 Asunción UFO landing claim is one of [Paraguay]({{ 'what-strange-stories-haunt-paraguay/' | relative_url }})’s best-known modern UFO stories: a reported object allegedly descended into the grounds of the Colegio Nacional de la Capital in Asunción during the early hours of February 1963. The account became famous because it was presented not simply as a distant light in the sky, but as a close encounter involving a school site, named witnesses, and later references in international UFO catalogues. Yet the physical evidence is limited: there are no verified photographs, recovered materials, or independently confirmed traces from the alleged landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://delparaguay.com.py/nota/los-extraterrestres-visitan-paraguay-el-supuesto-ovni-que-habria-aterrizado-en-el-cnc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: delparaguay.com.py">[Del Paraguay]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">delparaguay.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Del ParaguayDel Paraguay &#124; NotaMay 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 26, 2026</span></span></span>

The case remains interesting because it sits in the space between a remembered event and a documented investigation. ## The reported school incident

According to later Paraguayan UFO researchers, the incident took place in the early morning of 5 February 1963 at the Colegio Nacional de la Capital in Asunción. The central witness was identified in UFO literature as a student named Anastasio (or Atanasio) Leiva, who was reportedly on the school grounds and saw an unusual object rise from the area before disappearing into the sky. The case was later listed in UFO chronologies as a school-ground landing report from “Ascension, Paraguay”, reflecting an English-language spelling variation of Asunción.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufocasebook.com/magoniacomplete.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufocasebook.com">[UFO Casebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufocasebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Casebook Passport to Magonia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO CasebookPassport to Magonia - 100 Years of UFO Landings...</span></span></span>

Later retellings describe Leiva as having gone to the school grounds after losing a football, where he supposedly saw a strange craft with bright flashes lifting away. Some versions of the story add that the object produced orange or reddish light effects and moved away at great speed. These details come mainly from later UFO publications and compilations rather than surviving contemporary technical investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://es.scribd.com/document/941264951/Entrevista-Ronald-Maidana-en-UFo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: es.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">es.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Entrevista sobre Ufología en Paraguay &#124; PDF &#124; Objeto volador no identificado &#124; Ufología...</span></span></span>

The setting helped make the story memorable. A reported landing at a school carries a strong narrative impact because it suggests a physical location that could, in theory, be checked afterwards. However, the case does not appear to have produced the kind of evidence that would normally strengthen a landing claim: no confirmed landing marks, photographs, debris, radar records, or material samples are publicly associated with the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://delparaguay.com.py/nota/los-extraterrestres-visitan-paraguay-el-supuesto-ovni-que-habria-aterrizado-en-el-cnc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: delparaguay.com.py">[Del Paraguay]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">delparaguay.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Del ParaguayDel Paraguay &#124; NotaMay 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 26, 2026</span></span></span>

## Witness accounts and later investigations

The Asunción case gained attention partly because it was not presented as a single isolated sighting. Later accounts connected the school incident with other observations in the area, including reports from residents who allegedly saw an unusual object moving over the city. One account cited by Paraguayan UFO writer Ronald Maidana describes multiple witnesses, including a Ministry of the Interior official, reporting a fast-moving unidentified object around the same period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://delparaguay.com.py/nota/los-extraterrestres-visitan-paraguay-el-supuesto-ovni-que-habria-aterrizado-en-el-cnc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: delparaguay.com.py">[Del Paraguay]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">delparaguay.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Del ParaguayDel Paraguay &#124; NotaMay 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 26, 2026</span></span></span>

The case was also preserved through international UFO record-keeping. The United States Air Force’s Project Blue Book files contain a reference to an early February 1963 Asunción report, describing a student who saw an object land on a school sports field and noting additional sightings by residents, including a government official. The entry categorised the case as “No Case (Information Only)”, meaning it was recorded as information but did not become a fully investigated Blue Book case with a formal explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/1963.02%20-%209316772%20-%20Winslow%20Arizona.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[Bluebook Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1963.02 9316772 Winslow Arizona</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bluebook Files1963-02-9316772-Winslow-Arizona...</span></span></span>

Later UFO researchers used these references to argue that the incident deserves attention because it appears in historical records beyond local storytelling. However, inclusion in a catalogue does not confirm that the event occurred as described. UFO databases often preserve reports of unknown origin precisely because they document claims, not because they prove extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/chronos/1963fullrep.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1963 UFO ChronologyMay 14, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 14, 2006</span></span></span>

## Evidence debates and explanations

The strongest evidence for the Asunción UFO claim is the consistency of the reported story across later accounts: a named witness, a specific location, a specific date, and references from UFO researchers and archival collections. For researchers interested in the history of UFO reports, this makes it a valuable example of how a local incident can enter wider international UFO culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/chronos/1963fullrep.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1963 UFO ChronologyMay 14, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 14, 2006</span></span></span>

The weakest part of the case is the lack of physical evidence. Unlike some famous UFO reports that involve alleged photographs, instrument readings, multiple independent recordings, or objects that can be examined, the Asunción claim depends largely on testimony preserved through later retellings. There is no publicly available chain of evidence showing that a craft landed, left marks, or left behind material that could be tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://delparaguay.com.py/nota/los-extraterrestres-visitan-paraguay-el-supuesto-ovni-que-habria-aterrizado-en-el-cnc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: delparaguay.com.py">[Del Paraguay]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">delparaguay.com.py</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Del ParaguayDel Paraguay &#124; NotaMay 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 26, 2026</span></span></span>

Several ordinary explanations remain possible. A bright aerial object could have been a misidentified aircraft, astronomical object, atmospheric phenomenon, or another conventional source seen under unusual conditions. Memory can also reshape unusual experiences over time, especially when stories are repeated through newspapers, books, and UFO communities. These possibilities do not prove that witnesses were mistaken, but they show why unusual claims require more than sincere testimony to establish what happened.

The enduring appeal of the 1963 Asunción story lies less in a solved mystery than in its role as a piece of Paraguay’s strange-history record. It shows how a single dramatic report can survive for decades when it combines a memorable setting, human testimony, and unanswered questions. The evidence remains too limited to confirm an extraordinary event, but the case continues to illustrate how UFO stories are created, preserved, debated, and woven into national folklore.
