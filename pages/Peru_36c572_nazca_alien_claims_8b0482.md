## Introduction

Nazca’s “alien mummies” are one of Peru’s most unusual modern archaeological controversies: a story where ancient remains, grave looting, UFO culture, forensic science and viral media collided. The claims centre on small humanoid bodies with three fingers, elongated skulls and unusual anatomy that some researchers and media figures have presented as evidence of non-human beings. Archaeologists and Peruvian authorities, however, argue that the objects are either manipulated human remains, modern constructions, or a mixture of archaeological material and fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/arqueologicas/article/view/448" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistas.cultura.gob.pe">[Revistas Cultura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistas.cultura.gob.pe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revistas CulturaAnatomía de un fraude: el caso de las presuntas momias alienígenas humanoides tridáctilas de Nazca, Perú &#124; ArqueológicasD...</span></span></span>

The dispute matters because Nazca already occupies a special place in popular mystery culture. The ancient Nazca Lines — enormous designs carved into the desert — have long inspired speculation about lost technology and extraterrestrial involvement, even though archaeological research overwhelmingly treats them as products of the Nazca people’s own religious and cultural practices. The mummy controversy added a new layer: instead of asking why ancient Peruvians created extraordinary monuments, it shifted attention towards claims that Peru’s past might contain evidence of visitors from elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/nasca-lines" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

## The rise of the alien mummy claims

The modern “Nazca alien” story developed around alleged tridactyl (three-fingered) mummified bodies reported from the Nazca and Palpa areas of southern Peru. In the mid-2010s, French researcher Thierry Jamin and the Inkari Cusco Institute promoted claims that unusual desiccated bodies recovered in the region might represent unknown beings rather than altered human remains. The specimens were given names such as María, Josefina and Wawita, and images of their strange appearance spread rapidly online.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thierryjamin.com/2016-2019-alien-project-survol-montagne-carree/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thierryjamin.com">[Thierry Jamin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thierryjamin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2016 2019 alien project survol montagne carree</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Thierry Jamin2016-2019 - Alien Project - Survol de la montagne carrée...</span></span></span>

The claims gained international attention in 2023 when Mexican journalist and UFO promoter Jaime Maussan presented two small bodies at a hearing in the Mexican Congress, describing them as “non-human” specimens found in Peru. The event brought the subject into mainstream headlines, but also attracted criticism from scientists who argued that the presentation bypassed normal archaeological standards for studying ancient remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/af7d54fabf3278ef83c39d899c457c76" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The bodies, found in 2017, were allegedly discovered in the Peruvian coastal desert of Nazca. ## Archaeological objections and evidence

The central criticism from archaeologists is that the bodies have not produced reliable evidence of an unknown species. Instead, several investigations have concluded that at least some of the objects presented as “alien” are constructed artefacts made from earthly materials. A 2023 article in the Peruvian archaeological journal *Arqueológicas* described the case as a modern fraud involving manipulated remains and the way popular culture shapes ideas of extraterrestrial beings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/arqueologicas/article/view/448" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistas.cultura.gob.pe">[Revistas Cultura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistas.cultura.gob.pe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revistas CulturaAnatomía de un fraude: el caso de las presuntas momias alienígenas humanoides tridáctilas de Nazca, Perú &#124; ArqueológicasD...</span></span></span>

In January 2024, specialists from Peru’s Institute of Legal Medicine and Forensic Sciences examined two humanoid figures and a three-fingered hand seized at Jorge Chávez International Airport while they were reportedly being sent to Mexico. The Peruvian Ministry of Culture stated that the objects were not genuine ancient bodies but constructions made with materials including animal bones, human material, paper, synthetic adhesives and other modern components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gob.pe/institucion/cultura/noticias/893041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gob.pe">[Gobierno del Perú]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gob.pe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ierno del PerúEspecialistas del Ministerio Público confirmaron que momias incautadas en el aeropuerto Jorge Chávez no son reales - Not...</span></span></span>

The investigators highlighted several problems:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Mixed anatomy:** Some examined objects contained bones arranged in ways that did not represent a natural skeleton.
* **Modern materials:** Synthetic glues and construction methods were considered evidence against ancient biological origin.
* **Archaeological context:** The objects lacked the controlled excavation records normally required for a major archaeological discovery.
* **Heritage concerns:** The movement of questionable remains outside Peru raised wider issues about illegal excavation and trafficking of cultural material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gob.pe/institucion/cultura/noticias/893041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gob.pe">[Gobierno del Perú]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gob.pe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ierno del PerúEspecialistas del Ministerio Público confirmaron que momias incautadas en el aeropuerto Jorge Chávez no son reales - Not...</span></span></span>

The controversy is complicated because “Nazca mummies” does not describe one single object. Different specimens have been promoted by different groups, and supporters argue that criticism of some seized figures does not automatically resolve every claimed body. This distinction is important: proving that one object is fake does not by itself prove that every unusual specimen is identical. At the same time, extraordinary claims about unknown species require unusually strong evidence, and that standard has not been met in accepted archaeology.

## Why Nazca became linked with aliens

The alien mummy story did not appear in isolation. Nazca had already become one of the world’s most famous mystery landscapes because of its giant desert geoglyphs, which can be fully appreciated from the air. Early popular writers, especially those associated with ancient astronaut ideas, argued that the scale and precision of the lines suggested outside help. Archaeologists instead interpret them as human creations connected with ritual, landscape, water and religious practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/nasca-lines" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

This existing reputation made Nazca a powerful setting for a new mystery. A strange-looking body discovered in the same region as enormous desert drawings seemed, to some audiences, to fit a familiar story: an ancient civilisation supposedly connected with visitors from the stars. The archaeological reality is less dramatic but arguably more interesting. Nazca was already a place where people created remarkable things, preserved bodies through unusual desert conditions and developed complex beliefs about the relationship between humans, nature and the cosmos.

The alien interpretation often depends on a misunderstanding of how archaeology works. A mystery is not the same as evidence for an extraordinary explanation. Ancient cultures frequently produced objects that appear unfamiliar today, but unfamiliarity alone does not indicate an external origin.

## How modern myths spread

The Nazca alien controversy shows how quickly uncertain discoveries can move from archaeological curiosity to global spectacle. Social media rewards striking images, and a small body with a strange appearance is naturally more shareable than a careful discussion about conservation, excavation methods or forensic anatomy.

Several forces helped the story grow:

**Visual impact:** The bodies looked unlike ordinary human remains, making photographs highly memorable.

**Existing UFO culture:** Decades of ancient astronaut theories had already created an audience interested in links between ancient sites and extraterrestrials.

**Scientific language:** References to DNA testing, carbon dating and laboratory analysis gave the debate a scientific appearance, even when interpretations of those results remained disputed.

**Real archaeological vulnerability:** Peru genuinely does face problems with looting and illegal trade in ancient remains. This real issue became entangled with the fictional or unsupported claim that looted material represented alien evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/alien-fever-dreams-fuel-peruvian-grave-robbings-2024-04-06/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: reuters.com">[Reuters]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">reuters.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nazca, known for its ancient geoglyphs and archaeological significance, is prone to grave robbing. Rivera was convicted in 2022 for unear...</span></span></span>

The case also demonstrates why archaeological context matters. A genuine ancient discovery is not just an unusual object; it is a piece of evidence connected to a place, layer of soil, associated objects, burial practices and historical setting. Removing an object from that context can make it harder to understand and easier to reinterpret.

## Mystery or misinterpretation?

The Nazca alien claims remain culturally significant because they sit at the meeting point between real archaeology and modern mythology. Peru’s ancient past genuinely contains extraordinary achievements: monumental geoglyphs, sophisticated societies and remarkably preserved remains. Those facts do not require extraterrestrial explanations.

The strongest evidence available so far supports a story about human activity rather than alien visitors: unusual remains, modern manipulation, disputed interpretations and a powerful media cycle. The enduring mystery is therefore not whether aliens were buried in the Nazca desert, but why modern audiences continue to transform archaeological uncertainty into stories of other worlds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/arqueologicas/article/view/448" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: revistas.cultura.gob.pe">[Revistas Cultura]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">revistas.cultura.gob.pe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revistas CulturaAnatomía de un fraude: el caso de las presuntas momias alienígenas humanoides tridáctilas de Nazca, Perú &#124; ArqueológicasD...</span></span></span>

Nazca’s alien controversy has become part of Peru’s strange-history record precisely because it reveals something familiar across Fortean subjects: the most fascinating stories often emerge in the gap between what was found, what people hoped it meant, and what careful investigation can actually show.
