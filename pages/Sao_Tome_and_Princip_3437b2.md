## Why the islands became a natural home for strange stories

### Ruined plantations and the atmosphere of the unknown

The abandoned roas are among the most powerful sources of modern mystery around So Tom and Prncipe. During the colonial period, plantations became almost self-contained worlds, with workers quarters, hospitals, churches, administrative buildings and processing facilities. After independence in 1975, many estates declined, leaving behind empty structures slowly reclaimed by vegetation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitprincipe.com/beach/praia-banana/78-about/103-plantation.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitprincipe.com">[Visit Prncipe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitprincipe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visit Prncipe Principe Plantations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit PrncipePrincipe Plantations - The Island of Prncipe Guide - Visit Principe...</span></span></span>

To visitors, these locations can appear almost designed for ghost stories: huge empty buildings in tropical forest, rusting machinery, overgrown paths and reminders of a difficult past. Some popular accounts describe abandoned roas as haunted or connected with spirits, but the evidence is cultural rather than investigative. The haunting quality often comes from the very real human suffering associated with plantation life, including forced labour systems and colonial exploitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visitsaotomeprincipe.st/en/node/51" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsaotomeprincipe.st">[Visit So Tom and Prncipe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsaotomeprincipe.st</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsaotomeprincipe.st.</span></span></span>

This is an important distinction in understanding island Forteana. A ruined plantation does not need a confirmed ghost sighting to become a strange place. The mystery may lie in unanswered questions about forgotten lives, lost communities and how landscapes preserve memories.

## What folklore and traditions add to the mystery

So Tom and Prncipes cultural traditions combine Portuguese influences with African heritage developed through centuries of migration, colonial rule and plantation society. Oral storytelling, religious practices and performance traditions preserve ideas about justice, spirits, morality and the relationship between humans and the unseen world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iexplore.com/articles/travel-guides/africa/sao-tome-and-principe/history-and-culture" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iexplore.com">[iExplore]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iexplore.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">i Explore Sa Tme and Prncipe History and Culture</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">i Explore Sa Tme and Prncipe History and Culture</span></span></span>

One of the best-known cultural forms is Tchiloli, a traditional open-air theatre that presents a dramatic story involving a murder trial, kingship and justice. Although it is not a supernatural tradition, its ritual atmosphere and mixture of European dramatic elements with local performance practices show how unusual and symbolic storytelling remains central to island culture. UNESCO recognised Tchiloli as intangible cultural heritage in 2025.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.unesco.org/en/intangible-cultural-heritage/committee-2025?hub=779" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unesco.org">[UNESCO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unesco.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">committee 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intangible Cultural Heritage Committee 2025 &#124; UNESCOOctober 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 23, 2025</span></span></span>

Stories connected with spirits, magic and mysterious forces are part of the broader folklore environment of the islands. Such accounts are best understood as living traditions: ways communities explain danger, morality, memory and the unknown. They are not evidence that paranormal events occurred, but they are valuable records of how people interpret the world around them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://visitsaotomeprincipe.st/en/node/51" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitsaotomeprincipe.st">[Visit So Tom and Prncipe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitsaotomeprincipe.st</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on visitsaotomeprincipe.st.</span></span></span>

## The islands most famous impossible event was actually scientific

Not all strange stories from So Tom and Prncipe belong to folklore. One of the most remarkable events connected with the islands was a genuine scientific mystery solved through observation.

On 29 May 1919, the island of Prncipe became one of the locations where astronomers observed a total solar eclipse to test Albert Einsteins newly proposed theory of general relativity. At Roa Sundy, British astronomer Arthur Eddingtons expedition photographed stars near the eclipsed Sun and measured whether their apparent positions shifted because of the Suns gravity. The result became one of the most famous confirmations of Einsteins theory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08354" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Einstein and Eddington and the eclipse in Principe: Celebration and science 100 years afterDecember 18, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2019</span></span></span>

At the time, the idea that gravity could bend light seemed almost as strange as any supernatural claim. The episode is a useful reminder that unexplained does not always mean paranormal. Some of historys strangest moments are eventually explained through better instruments, careful observation and improved understanding.
