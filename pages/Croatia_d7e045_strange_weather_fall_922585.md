## Introduction

[Croatia]({{ 'croatias-strangest-stories-between/' | relative_url }})’s most memorable “impossible weather” stories are not usually about the sky producing something truly unknown, but about ordinary natural forces creating scenes that seem impossible at first glance. Reports of animals falling with rain, objects apparently dropping from clouds, or violent storms changing the landscape belong to a wider tradition of strange weather accounts found around the world. In Croatia, the strongest connection comes from the country’s storm-prone Adriatic coast, where waterspouts, sudden downpours and powerful convective storms can create conditions that make unusual events seem almost unreal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016980950600127X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Waterspouts of the Adriatic, Ionian and Aegean Sea...by MV Sioutas · 2007 · Cited by 57 — The waterspouts were reported wit...</span></span></span>

## How storms can transport animals

### When the sky appears to rain living creatures

“Animal falls” are among the strangest reported weather phenomena. They describe occasions when fish, frogs, small crustaceans or other animals are found scattered after a storm, leading witnesses to conclude that they have fallen from the sky. Similar reports have appeared in many countries over centuries, but the evidence varies greatly: some are carefully documented, while others survive mainly as newspaper stories or local traditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_of_animals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rain of animals</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rain of animals</span></span></span>

The leading explanation involves waterspouts and intense storm updrafts. A waterspout is a rotating column of air over water, similar in appearance to a tornado. Under the right conditions, strong winds and rising air can lift small animals from shallow coastal waters, ponds or flooded areas. If the storm weakens or changes direction, the material carried inside the circulation may fall elsewhere. Scientists consider this a plausible explanation for many reports of fish and frogs appearing after storms, although directly observing the complete transport process remains extremely difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/everyday-mysteries/meteorology-climatology/item/can-it-rain-frogs-fish-and-other-objects/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressCan it rain frogs, fish, and other objects?Many scientists believe tornadic waterspouts may be responsible for fro...</span></span></span>

Croatia’s geography makes this mechanism particularly relevant. The Adriatic Sea is well known for waterspout activity, especially during periods when warm sea temperatures meet unstable air masses. Research into Adriatic waterspouts has documented numerous events and found that the region regularly experiences both fair-weather waterspouts and more intense storm-related forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016980950600127X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Waterspouts of the Adriatic, Ionian and Aegean Sea...by MV Sioutas · 2007 · Cited by 57 — The waterspouts were reported wit...</span></span></span>

A storm over the Adriatic therefore creates the right ingredients for a genuinely strange-looking event: a visible funnel over the sea, powerful winds, sudden rainfall and the possibility of material being moved from one place to another. Even when no animals are involved, such storms can appear extraordinary enough to inspire folklore and speculation.

## Reports of unusual falls and reactions

### Croatia’s documented “strange weather” is mostly about storms

Waterspouts are among the clearest examples. In January 2016, researchers examined a tornadic waterspout event in Split, analysing the atmospheric conditions that produced the phenomenon. ### Why animal-fall stories spread so easily

Animal falls occupy an interesting middle ground between science and folklore. They are unusual enough to attract attention but often leave gaps in the evidence. A person who discovers fish, frogs or other small animals after a storm may reasonably describe what they saw, while the later interpretation — “they fell from the sky” — becomes a separate question.

Several factors make these reports memorable:

## Science behind rare natural events

### The Adriatic as a laboratory for strange weather

Croatia’s coastline is an ideal place to study dramatic weather because it combines a warm enclosed sea with complex coastal terrain. Mountains rise close to the Adriatic, while weather systems moving across the region can create sharp contrasts in temperature and pressure.

Waterspouts are especially important to the Croatian strange-weather record because they demonstrate how familiar physical processes can look extraordinary. A rotating column of air over the sea may appear like a bridge between ocean and cloud, yet it is governed by the same atmospheric principles as other severe storms. Research on eastern Adriatic waterspout forecasting has examined how instability, wind changes with height and local conditions contribute to their formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-11958" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: link.springer.com">[Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">link.springer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Waterspout Forecasting Method Over the Eastern Adriatic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Waterspout Forecasting Method Over the Eastern Adriatic...July 7, 2019 — by T Renko · 2019 · Cited by 17 — In this study, a syno...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2019</span></span></span>

This helps explain why reports of unusual falls should not immediately be treated as evidence of something unexplained. The atmosphere is capable of moving water, ice, dust, plants and occasionally small animals in ways that humans rarely witness directly.

### Strange weather without supernatural causes

Fortean interest in these events comes from the tension between appearance and explanation. A storm that seems to violate common sense often turns out to reveal something unfamiliar about natural systems.

For Croatia, the most valuable lesson from unusual weather reports is not that the country has proven cases of impossible events, but that its environment regularly produces conditions where the impossible-looking can happen. A waterspout near an island, giant hail after a summer storm, or an unexpected object carried by violent winds all show the same pattern: nature can be surprising without needing to break natural laws.

The enduring appeal of these stories comes from that uncertainty. Before investigation, a strange fall from the sky can look like a mystery. After investigation, it often becomes something equally fascinating — a rare example of how powerful and complicated ordinary weather can be.
