## Introduction

El Cajas, the windswept highland west of Cuenca, is one of [Ecuador]({{ 'where-ecuadors-weird-stories-touch-real/' | relative_url }})'s most atmospheric landscapes and one of its richest sources of strange stories. More than 3,000 metres above sea level, the national park is a maze of glacial lakes, rolling páramo grassland, twisted forests and fast-changing weather. In such conditions, mist, unusual light effects and the psychological impact of altitude naturally encourage unusual experiences. Around those natural conditions have grown a remarkable mixture of Marian pilgrimage, UFO watching and local monster tales. None of these traditions proves anything supernatural, but together they show how one dramatic landscape can become a meeting point for faith, folklore and modern mystery.

Unlike many famous paranormal locations, El Cajas is interesting precisely because its stories overlap. A pilgrim may describe a religious vision, a hiker may report mysterious lights, while UFO enthusiasts interpret the same landscape as a hotspot for extraterrestrial activity. The mountain itself remains constant; only the explanations differ.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cajas_National_Park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Cajas National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El Cajas National Park</span></span></span>

## Why this landscape encourages unusual experiences

El Cajas National Park occupies a high Andean plateau between roughly 3,100 and 4,450 metres above sea level. Hundreds of glacial lakes, exposed ridges and dense banks of cloud create rapidly changing visibility. Sunshine can vanish into fog within minutes before breaking through again in dramatic shafts of light.

These conditions matter because they provide perfectly ordinary mechanisms for extraordinary-looking events. Moisture, ice crystals and drifting cloud can produce brilliant reflections, halos, sudden bright patches and shifting silhouettes. Distances are notoriously difficult to judge across the open páramo, while thin air, cold and fatigue can affect perception, particularly for visitors unused to high altitude. Experienced hikers often remark on how quickly familiar landmarks disappear into mist before reappearing from unexpected directions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cajas_National_Park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">El Cajas National Park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El Cajas National Park</span></span></span>

For Fortean enthusiasts, this does not explain every report, but it does explain why El Cajas repeatedly generates sincere eyewitness accounts without requiring deliberate hoaxes or mass deception.

## Fog, altitude and difficult sightings

Many reports from El Cajas describe lights rather than structured objects. Witnesses have spoken of glowing points moving over ridges, sudden flashes near lakes or luminous forms seen through drifting fog.

Several factors make such observations difficult to interpret:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Vehicle headlights can appear unexpectedly over distant mountain roads before disappearing behind terrain.
* Temperature inversions and moisture can refract light in unusual ways.
* Reflections from lakes may exaggerate brightness or apparent movement.
* Low cloud frequently obscures reference points, making stationary lights seem to move.
* Altitude, exhaustion and isolation can encourage mistaken estimates of distance and speed.

</div>

Because sightings are often brief and occur in poor visibility, they rarely produce evidence that can distinguish confidently between unusual atmospheric phenomena and genuinely unidentified objects. This ambiguity has helped keep El Cajas within Ecuador's UFO folklore long after individual reports faded from the news.

Unlike classic UFO waves supported by multiple radar records or official investigations, El Cajas is remembered more as a place where unusual experiences are expected. ## Marian devotion turned El Cajas into a pilgrimage landscape

The area's best-known visionary tradition is not about aliens but about religion.

Beginning in the late 1980s, Patricia "Pachi" Talbot reported repeated apparitions of the Virgin Mary near El Cajas. According to supporters, the messages encouraged prayer, repentance and renewed Catholic faith. Large crowds travelled into the mountains to attend prayer meetings, and many pilgrims claimed unusual accompanying phenomena, including striking solar effects reminiscent of reports from other famous Marian apparition sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guardianadelafe.com/en/contenido/historia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guardianadelafe.com">[guardianadelafe.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guardianadelafe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mary, Mother Guardian of the FaithOn December 8, 2002, Monsignor Vicente Cisneros Durán declared this very special place, &quot;El Cajas&quot;, an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2002</span></span></span>

These experiences remain controversial. The reported apparitions have devoted followers, while the wider Catholic Church has treated claims of supernatural visions with its usual caution. Regardless of one's view of the apparitions themselves, the movement transformed El Cajas into an important regional pilgrimage destination.

The Sanctuary of the Virgin Mary, Guardian of the Faith now stands near the reported apparition site, and regular pilgrimages continue. The existence of an active shrine demonstrates the lasting cultural impact of the visions even though the supernatural claims remain matters of personal belief rather than established historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://guardianadelafe.com/en/contenido/historia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: guardianadelafe.com">[guardianadelafe.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">guardianadelafe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mary, Mother Guardian of the FaithOn December 8, 2002, Monsignor Vicente Cisneros Durán declared this very special place, &quot;El Cajas&quot;, an...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2002</span></span></span>

## UFO tourism and the Blue Llama

Alongside religious pilgrimage, El Cajas has developed a reputation among UFO enthusiasts.

Local guides have long promoted particular viewpoints where unusual lights are said to appear. Visitors sometimes organise night watches on isolated ridges, hoping to witness unexplained aerial phenomena. Although convincing physical evidence has never emerged, the expectation of seeing something unusual has become part of the area's tourist identity.

A recent example is *The Blue Llama: Secret Message*, a documentary following a group searching for alleged extraterrestrial encounters around a landmark known as Devil's Wart Mountain. The production mixes humour, local folklore and genuine curiosity, reflecting the modern character of El Cajas mythology rather than presenting confirmed evidence of alien activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://astrolabioproducciones.com/2020/08/09/encuentro-cuencano-del-tercer-tipo-ecuadorian-encounters-of-the-third-type/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astrolabioproducciones.com">[astrolabioproducciones.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astrolabioproducciones.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Blue Llama Secret Message</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">El viaje hacia las ideasAugust 9, 2020 — 9 Aug 2020 — En noviembre de 2020, en medio de una pandemia mundial, un grupo de 8 jubilados nor...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 9, 2020</span></span></span>

The "Blue Llama" itself illustrates how contemporary folklore evolves. Rather than being an ancient indigenous legend, it represents a playful modern addition to older mountain mystery traditions. Stories circulate through documentaries, local media and tourism, gradually becoming part of the area's living folklore.

## The mountain monster and the blurred line between legend and testimony

El Cajas also hosts stories of an elusive mountain creature sometimes called the Guagua Grande or simply the Cajas Monster.

Witnesses have described a large, mysterious being seen in remote parts of the park. Some accounts mention unusual footprints, while others refer to fleeting glimpses of a dark figure moving through fog. Researchers and television crews have occasionally revisited these claims, but no evidence has persuaded zoologists or park authorities that an unknown animal inhabits the reserve.

What makes the stories interesting is not their evidential strength but their persistence. Park officials and university scientists have repeatedly stated that there is no solid proof for such creatures, yet reports continue to circulate among local residents and visitors alike. ## Why the stories endure

El Cajas succeeds as a centre of Ecuadorian Fortean tradition because it combines several ingredients rarely found together.

The physical environment already encourages uncertainty through cloud, altitude and shifting light. The Marian apparition tradition gives the landscape spiritual significance. UFO enthusiasts reinterpret unusual skies through a different framework, while monster stories supply an older folklore of hidden creatures in lonely mountains.

Each community approaches the same landscape differently:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Pilgrims** see a place touched by divine intervention.
* **UFO enthusiasts** view it as a potential window onto unexplained aerial phenomena.
* **Local storytellers** preserve tales of strange creatures and uncanny encounters.
* **Scientists and park authorities** generally point to weather, geography and psychology as sufficient explanations for most reports.

</div>

None of these perspectives has displaced the others. Instead, they coexist, making El Cajas one of Ecuador's most distinctive examples of how natural environments become repositories for multiple kinds of mystery.

## A landscape where explanation depends on expectation

El Cajas demonstrates that unusual places do not need a single defining mystery to become legendary. Instead, its reputation has grown because different traditions continually reinforce one another.

A walker who arrives expecting remarkable scenery will almost certainly find it. A pilgrim may experience profound religious emotion. A UFO watcher may interpret distant lights as something extraordinary. A sceptic may see only weather, optics and suggestion. All occupy the same mountains under the same skies.

That combination of spectacular geography, sincere testimony, limited evidence and enduring folklore explains why El Cajas remains one of Ecuador's most intriguing centres of strange stories. Its enduring mystery lies less in proving the paranormal than in showing how one dramatic landscape can inspire radically different interpretations while never entirely surrendering its sense of wonder.
