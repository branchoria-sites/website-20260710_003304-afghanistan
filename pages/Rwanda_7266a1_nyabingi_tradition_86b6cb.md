## Introduction

Nyabingi was not simply a supernatural figure in [Rwanda]({{ 'the-strange-stories-hidden-in-rwandas/' | relative_url }})’s past. The tradition surrounding Nyabingi (also written Nyabinghi or Nyavingi) was a regional spirit system that linked healing, fertility, personal protection, ritual authority and political influence. In northern Rwanda and nearby parts of the Great Lakes region, people sought help from Nyabingi through spirit mediums who were believed to communicate with the powerful female spirit. For historians, the tradition is significant because it sits at the meeting point of belief and history: it was a religious practice, a source of social authority and, at times, a language of resistance against political control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/cea_0008-0055_1974_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Persée Ritual and History: the Case of Nyabingi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeRitual and History: the Case of Nyabingi - Persée...</span></span></span>

The evidence for Nyabingi does not show a proven supernatural force in the modern scientific sense. Instead, historians study it as a living religious tradition whose followers understood spirits as real actors in everyday life. Its importance in Rwanda’s “strange history” comes from the way a spirit tradition became connected with questions of power, identity and opposition, leaving behind records that blur the boundary between folklore and documented political history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?DB=p&amp;RID=190085053" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>

## The spirit figure and its stories

Nyabingi was associated particularly with communities in northern Rwanda and the neighbouring Kigezi region of present-day Uganda. Historical studies describe her as a female divinity or spirit whose followers, known as bagirwa, sought assistance through ritual consultations and possession practices. Unlike some broader regional traditions centred on royal authority, Nyabingi worship developed strongly in areas where local communities and lineage groups held significant independence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/cea_0008-0055_1974_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Persée Ritual and History: the Case of Nyabingi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeRitual and History: the Case of Nyabingi - Persée...</span></span></span>

Stories about Nyabingi’s origins vary. Some traditions describe her as a powerful woman or royal figure whose identity continued after death through spiritual possession. Others present her more directly as a divinity whose presence could enter chosen mediums. Because the tradition was transmitted largely through oral histories and ritual practice, historians do not treat these origin stories as straightforward biographies. Instead, they examine how the stories preserved ideas about ancestry, authority, protection and social memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyabinghi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

A key feature of Nyabingi belief was the role of mediums. These individuals acted as intermediaries between ordinary people and the spirit, receiving offerings and conducting consultations. Followers might seek help with illness, fertility concerns, family problems or misfortune. To believers, the medium provided access to a powerful spiritual presence; to historians, the practice reveals how communities created systems of care and decision-making outside formal political institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/cea_0008-0055_1974_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Persée Ritual and History: the Case of Nyabingi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeRitual and History: the Case of Nyabingi - Persée...</span></span></span>

## Belief, authority and social change

Nyabingi became historically unusual because spiritual authority could become social authority. A person recognised as connected with the spirit could influence communities, attract followers and challenge existing power structures. This did not mean that every Nyabingi practitioner was a political leader, but the tradition provided a respected source of legitimacy that did not depend entirely on royal or colonial institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=03832587X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>

### The northern Rwanda connection

In Rwanda, Nyabingi traditions were especially associated with northern areas around Byumba and Ruhengeri. Research by anthropologist Jim Freedman argued that Nyabingi had a distinct place within local religious life, differing from the better-known kubandwa rituals associated with other spirit traditions in the region. Freedman noted that Nyabingi practices were more closely linked with private consultation and the history of specific communities rather than the symbolic support of an established political order.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/cea_0008-0055_1974_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Persée Ritual and History: the Case of Nyabingi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeRitual and History: the Case of Nyabingi - Persée...</span></span></span>

This made Nyabingi particularly important in areas where people negotiated relationships with larger states and outside authorities. The spirit tradition offered a way to express community identity and preserve local histories. The “strangeness” of Nyabingi, from a modern perspective, lies not only in claims of spirit communication but in the fact that those claims had practical social consequences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=03832587X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>

### Muhumusa and the politics of possession

The most famous historical figure connected with Nyabingi was Muhumusa (also spelled Muhumuza), a spiritual leader active around the turn of the twentieth century. She claimed authority through Nyabingi and became involved in opposition to colonial and royal authorities in the Great Lakes region. Her movement attracted followers who believed she possessed special spiritual power and political legitimacy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhumusa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Muhumusa’s career shows why Nyabingi cannot be treated only as folklore. Colonial officials recorded the movement as a political problem because spiritual loyalty could compete with government authority. Researchers have debated whether Nyabingi movements should be classified mainly as religious movements, anti-colonial resistance or broader struggles over local autonomy. The strongest interpretation is that they were all of these at once: spiritual beliefs provided the language through which social and political conflicts were expressed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=153786779" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>

## History versus supernatural interpretation

Nyabingi raises a recurring question in the study of unusual traditions: should such accounts be understood as belief, history or something unexplained? The answer depends on what is being investigated.

From a religious perspective, Nyabingi represents a powerful spiritual relationship between humans and the unseen world. Followers historically understood possession, healing and ritual consultation as genuine interactions with a living force. From a historical perspective, scholars focus on the human effects of those beliefs: how communities organised themselves, how leaders gained influence and how colonial authorities responded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.persee.fr/doc/cea_0008-0055_1974_num" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: persee.fr">[Persée]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">persee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Persée Ritual and History: the Case of Nyabingi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PerséeRitual and History: the Case of Nyabingi - Persée...</span></span></span>

Sceptical interpretations do not require dismissing the tradition as meaningless. Anthropologists generally examine spirit possession and ritual authority as social phenomena rather than testing whether spirits exist. The important evidence is therefore not a laboratory proof of Nyabingi’s existence, but the historical record showing that belief in Nyabingi shaped real decisions, alliances and conflicts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=03832587X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>

The colonial period also changed how Nyabingi was described. European officials and missionaries often interpreted indigenous spiritual movements through their own religious and political assumptions, sometimes viewing them as threats or as forms of superstition. Modern historians have revisited those records more cautiously, recognising that colonial reports captured genuine conflicts but also reflected colonial fears and priorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=153786779" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>

Nyabingi remains part of Rwanda’s wider strange-history landscape because it occupies an unusual middle ground. It is neither simply a ghost story nor only a political episode. It is a case where a spirit tradition became a source of healing, identity and resistance, showing how beliefs about unseen powers can leave visible marks on history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=03832587X" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: africabib.org">[AfricaBib]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">africabib.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on africabib.org.</span></span></span>
