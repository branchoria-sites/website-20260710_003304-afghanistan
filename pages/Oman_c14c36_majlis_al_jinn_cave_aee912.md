## Introduction

Majlis al Jinn, or the “Meeting Place of the Jinn”, is one of Oman’s most remarkable natural mysteries because it combines a real geological wonder with a name that sounds like something from legend. Hidden beneath the Selma Plateau in the eastern Hajar Mountains, the cave is a vast underground chamber created by natural processes, not a supernatural construction. Yet its enormous size, remote location, and connection with stories about jinn have given it a lasting place in Oman’s strange-history landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.majlisaljinncave.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majlisaljinncave.com">[Majlis al Jinn Cave]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majlisaljinncave.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn CaveMajlis al Jinn CaveThe cave is a single chamber stretching roughly 310 by 225 meters with a soaring ceiling up to 120...</span></span></span>

The mystery of Majlis al Jinn is therefore not whether the cave contains evidence of spirits, but why a perfectly natural place became associated with the unseen world. Its story sits at the meeting point between exploration, geology, local belief, and the human tendency to attach meaning to landscapes that seem too extraordinary to be ordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/6847458/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en-academic.com">[Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en-academic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Majlis al Jinn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Majlis al Jinn</span></span></span>

## The discovery and scale of Majlis al Jinn

Majlis al Jinn was brought to wider attention through geological exploration rather than a search for legends. In the early 1980s, American hydrogeologist W. Don Davison Jr. and his wife Cheryl S. Jones were working on Oman’s karst research programme, which investigated limestone landscapes and underground water resources. The entrances on the Selma Plateau were identified in 1983, and exploration began with rope descents into the huge chamber below.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_al_Jinn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majlis al Jinn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn</span></span></span>

The first descent took place through one of the ceiling openings known as the First Drop, followed by further exploration of the other entrances. Davison and Jones surveyed and photographed the chamber in 1985, producing one of the first detailed records of the cave. Their work transformed a hidden void in the mountains into one of the world’s best-known large cave chambers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_al_Jinn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majlis al Jinn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn</span></span></span>

The scale of Majlis al Jinn is what makes it extraordinary. The cave is a single enormous chamber measuring roughly 310 metres by 225 metres, with a ceiling reaching around 120 metres above the floor. The chamber is entered only through openings in the roof, creating the dramatic experience of descending from bright desert sunlight into a huge underground space.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.majlisaljinncave.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majlisaljinncave.com">[Majlis al Jinn Cave]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majlisaljinncave.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn CaveMajlis al Jinn CaveThe cave is a single chamber stretching roughly 310 by 225 meters with a soaring ceiling up to 120...</span></span></span>

Rather than a maze of tunnels like some famous cave systems, Majlis al Jinn is notable for its emptiness. The lack of extensive side passages adds to its unusual atmosphere: explorers are not travelling through a winding underground world but dropping into a vast hidden room carved inside the mountain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.showcaves.com/english/om/caves/Majlis.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: showcaves.com">[Show Caves of the World]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">showcaves.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Show Caves of the World Caves of Oman: Majlis Al-Jin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Show Caves of the World Caves of Oman: Majlis Al-Jin</span></span></span>

## Why the cave gained a mysterious reputation

The name Majlis al Jinn is one of the main reasons the cave became associated with mystery. The name refers to a gathering place of jinn, supernatural beings recognised in Islamic tradition and folklore. The name does not prove that unusual beings were ever present in the cave; instead, it reflects a cultural relationship between caves, remote places, and the unseen world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/6847458/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en-academic.com">[Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en-academic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Majlis al Jinn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Academic Dictionaries and Encyclopedias Majlis al Jinn</span></span></span>

According to accounts of the discovery, local people living around the Selma Plateau did not have a widely used name for the cave openings encountered by explorers. Cheryl Jones chose the name Majlis al Jinn because of local beliefs that caves could be associated with jinn. Later, some locals used another name, Khoshilat Maqandeli, linked to a goat shelter near one of the entrances, while Majlis al Jinn became the internationally recognised name.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_al_Jinn" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majlis al Jinn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn</span></span></span>

This naming history shows how strange reputations develop. A dramatic landscape does not need a paranormal event to become mysterious. A deep opening in the earth, unfamiliar sounds, darkness, and the difficulty of entering a place can create a powerful sense of the unknown. Across many cultures, caves have been imagined as gateways, hiding places, or locations where humans encounter forces beyond everyday experience.

Majlis al Jinn is particularly suited to this kind of storytelling because of its physical characteristics. A person standing on the surface sees only small openings in the rock, yet beneath them lies a chamber large enough to hold a building-sized space. The contrast between the ordinary-looking landscape above and the enormous hidden void below naturally encourages wonder.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.majlisaljinncave.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majlisaljinncave.com">[Majlis al Jinn Cave]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majlisaljinncave.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn CaveMajlis al Jinn CaveThe cave is a single chamber stretching roughly 310 by 225 meters with a soaring ceiling up to 120...</span></span></span>

## Geology versus folklore in Oman’s landscapes

The scientific explanation for Majlis al Jinn is less mysterious but no less impressive. The cave formed in limestone through geological processes involving water movement and the gradual development of underground cavities. Rainwater and groundwater can dissolve soluble rock over long periods, enlarging cracks and weaknesses until large chambers form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/majlis-al-jinn-s9t7cj/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Expedition &#124; The Amazing Geology Behind Majlis al Jinn &#124; Digital Extra &#124; Season 1 &#124; PBS...</span></span></span>

The cave is part of Oman’s wider karst landscape, where limestone formations create dramatic underground spaces, sinkholes, and cliffs. These landscapes often appear almost artificial because their shapes are produced by slow processes operating over immense periods of time. The “mystery” is therefore one of scale and timescale rather than unexplained forces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/majlis-al-jinn-s9t7cj/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Expedition &#124; The Amazing Geology Behind Majlis al Jinn &#124; Digital Extra &#124; Season 1 &#124; PBS...</span></span></span>

The folklore surrounding Majlis al Jinn does not have to compete with geology. The two explanations answer different questions. Geology explains how the chamber formed; folklore explains why humans attach stories and meanings to places like it.

This overlap is central to Oman’s wider strange-history tradition. Places associated with jinn, spirits, unusual sounds, or hidden worlds often reveal how communities interpret dramatic environments. A remote cave can simultaneously be a scientific object, an adventure destination, and a place carrying cultural memories.

## Exploration, danger and the modern legend

Entering Majlis al Jinn is an expedition rather than a normal cave visit. The chamber can only be reached by descending through the ceiling openings, with some drops extending well over 100 metres. The difficulty of access helped preserve the cave’s aura and made exploration a specialist activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.majlisaljinncave.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majlisaljinncave.com">[Majlis al Jinn Cave]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majlisaljinncave.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majlis al Jinn CaveMajlis al Jinn CaveThe cave is a single chamber stretching roughly 310 by 225 meters with a soaring ceiling up to 120...</span></span></span>

Modern exploration has added new layers to the cave’s reputation. Images of climbers descending into the darkness, surrounded by enormous rock walls, helped turn Majlis al Jinn into an international symbol of extreme cave adventure. Coverage by outlets including National Geographic introduced the chamber to a global audience interested in underground exploration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/photography/article/cave-exploration" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalgeographic.com">[National Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalgeographic.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationalgeographic.com.</span></span></span>

However, the cave’s popularity also created practical challenges. A fragile underground environment can be damaged by careless visitors, and access requires safety controls because of the heights involved. Oman has introduced permit arrangements for entering Majlis al Jinn, and plans have been discussed for carefully managed tourism development that balances adventure access with environmental protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gov.om/en/w/get-permit-to-enter-majlis-ai-jinn-cave" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gov.om">[Gov.om]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gov.om</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Get Permit to Enter Majlis AI Jinn Cave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">11, 2026...</span></span></span>

## What the cave tells us about mystery in Oman

Majlis al Jinn remains one of Oman’s most compelling examples of a place where mystery comes from the interaction between nature and imagination. There is no reliable evidence that the cave is a supernatural location, but there is strong evidence that it is an exceptional geological formation with a name and atmosphere shaped by cultural ideas about hidden worlds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/majlis-al-jinn-s9t7cj/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Expedition &#124; The Amazing Geology Behind Majlis al Jinn &#124; Digital Extra &#124; Season 1 &#124; PBS...</span></span></span>

Its importance lies in that combination. The cave demonstrates how a landscape can become mysterious without needing a proven anomaly. The physical reality is already extraordinary: a huge underground chamber, discovered through scientific exploration, concealed beneath a remote plateau. The folklore adds another layer, showing how humans have always used stories to interpret places that inspire awe.

In Oman’s record of unusual places and strange traditions, Majlis al Jinn is a rare case where the wonder is not created by a mystery replacing science, but by science revealing something that feels almost mythical.
