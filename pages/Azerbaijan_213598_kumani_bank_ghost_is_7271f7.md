## Introduction

The so-called Kumani Bank "ghost island" is one of [Azerbaijan]({{ 'why-azerbaijans-weirdness-starts-in-the/' | relative_url }})'s most striking natural curiosities because it really did appear, shrink and almost disappear again within a remarkably short period. Unlike many Fortean stories, the mystery is not whether the event happened but why such dramatic changes can occur in plain sight. The answer lies beneath the Caspian Sea, where the Kumani Bank mud volcano periodically erupts, throwing enough mud and sediment above the water to create a temporary island before waves and currents wear it away again. Satellite imagery from 2023 and 2024 has turned what might once have become maritime folklore into one of the best-documented examples of a disappearing island anywhere in the world.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

## The 2023 island emergence

In early 2023, the underwater mud volcano known as Kumani Bank, also called Chigil-Deniz, erupted roughly 25 kilometres off Azerbaijan's eastern coast. The eruption forced mud, rock fragments and gas through the seabed until enough material accumulated above sea level to form a new island. Landsat satellites recorded the transformation in remarkable detail, showing that the island emerged between the end of January and the beginning of February before rapidly growing to around 400 metres across.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

For anyone encountering only a single satellite photograph, the event looks almost supernatural. A patch of open sea suddenly becomes dry land. Yet the sequence of images removes much of the mystery. Rather than appearing instantaneously, the island grew as fresh sediment accumulated around the eruption site while muddy plumes spread into the surrounding water, revealing that geological processes were actively reshaping the seabed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

The episode attracted worldwide attention in early 2025 after NASA's Earth Observatory published a detailed comparison of satellite images, introducing many readers to a phenomenon that local geologists have recognised for generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why did it disappear so quickly?

The feature earned its "ghost island" nickname because it proved astonishingly short-lived. By the end of 2024, satellite observations showed that only a much smaller remnant remained above the sea surface. Wind, waves and Caspian currents steadily eroded the unconsolidated mud deposited during the eruption until most of the island vanished beneath the water once more.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

This rapid disappearance is exactly what geologists expect from mud-volcano islands. Unlike volcanic islands built from solidified lava, Kumani Bank consists largely of water-rich mud and soft sediments. These materials can accumulate quickly during an eruption but are equally vulnerable to erosion afterwards. The island was therefore never a permanent addition to Azerbaijan's coastline but a temporary geological landform.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

The satellite record is particularly valuable because it captures both stages of the process: creation and destruction. Earlier generations often relied on sailors' reports or occasional surveys, leaving room for exaggeration. Modern remote sensing documents the entire life cycle.

## What makes the evidence unusually strong?

Many Fortean stories survive because evidence is scarce. Kumani Bank is almost the opposite. The principal evidence comes from independent satellite observations rather than eyewitness testimony.

Several lines of evidence reinforce one another:

* **Repeated Landsat imagery** documents the island's appearance, maximum extent and gradual erosion over nearly two years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Visible sediment plumes** surrounding the island are consistent with an active mud-volcano eruption rather than an optical illusion or navigational error.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Historical geological records** show that Kumani Bank has produced temporary islands many times before, meaning the 2023 event fits a recognised pattern rather than being an isolated anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Recent scientific analysis** using high-resolution satellite data has reconstructed the island's dimensions and timeline, confirming that erosion rather than disappearance by some unexplained mechanism accounts for its fate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392162900_Transformation_of_an_Underwater_Bank_into_the_Kumani_Island_in_the_Southwestern_Part_of_the_Caspian_Sea" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Transformation of an Underwater Bank into the Kumani...20 May 2025 — The satellite data used have a high spatial resolution...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2025</span></span></span>

Because the evidence comes from multiple observations over time, there is little room for claims that the island was merely a photographic artefact or a mistaken report.

## Older Kumani Bank appearances

The 2023 island was dramatic but far from unique. Kumani Bank's first documented eruption producing an island dates to 1861. Contemporary accounts describe a small island rising above the sea before disappearing again within roughly a year. Since then, several additional eruptions have repeated the same pattern: the volcano briefly creates land before natural erosion reclaims it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/satellites-spot-a-ghost-island-153799/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Science Satellites Spot a “Ghost” Island</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceSatellites Spot a “Ghost” IslandJanuary 10, 2025 — 10 Jan 2025 — The Kumani Bank mud volcano in the Caspian Sea suddenly mani...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 10, 2025</span></span></span>

This recurring behaviour explains why the feature occupies an unusual place in Azerbaijan's strange-history tradition. Before satellite monitoring, sailors or fishermen might genuinely have believed an island had mysteriously appeared or vanished between voyages. Such stories could easily acquire legendary embellishments, especially in the vast, often mist-covered Caspian Sea.

Today, the geological explanation is well established, but the repeated cycle still feels uncanny. A place that exists one year, fades the next and may return decades later retains much of the romance traditionally associated with phantom islands.

## Why the ghost island still matters

Kumani Bank demonstrates that some of Azerbaijan's strangest stories become more fascinating, not less, once they are explained. The evidence shows that the island is neither a mirage nor a supernatural phenomenon.
