## Introduction

[Uruguay]({{ 'why-uruguay-takes-strange-reports/' | relative_url }}) is unusual among countries with a reputation for UFO sightings because it has maintained an official military body dedicated to receiving and investigating reports for more than four decades. That fact is often presented online as proof that governments know UFOs are extraterrestrial. The evidence points to a more interesting conclusion. Uruguay's Air Force files demonstrate that unusual aerial reports are considered worth documenting and investigating for aviation safety and airspace awareness. They also show that, after careful investigation, most reports receive conventional explanations, while a small minority remain unexplained because the available evidence is insufficient to identify them with confidencenot because they have been proved to involve alien spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.segurilatam.com/entrevistas/coronel-ariel-sanchez-rios-cridovni-la-reciente-respuesta-de-estados-unidos-contra-ovnis-se-ajusta-a-su-doctrina-de-defensa_20230309.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: segurilatam.com">[Seguritecnia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">segurilatam.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Seguritecnia Cridovni se pronuncia sobre los derribos de Estados Unidos a ovnis</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SeguritecniaCridovni se pronuncia sobre los derribos de Estados Unidos a ovnisMarch 9, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2023</span></span></span>

For readers interested in Uruguay's Fortean history, the significance of these files lies less in spectacular claims than in the existence of a long-running official archive. Rather than relying solely on folklore or sensational headlines, Uruguay offers a rare opportunity to examine how a military organisation handles reports of strange things seen in its skies.

## Why CRIDOVNI was created

Uruguay's official UFO investigations are carried out by **CRIDOVNI** (the Commission for the Reception and Investigation of Reports of Unidentified Flying Objects), established by the Uruguayan Air Force on 7 August 1979. The commission was created during a period when increasing numbers of reports prompted concern that unidentified aerial objects might represent hazards to aviation or national airspace. Its purpose was therefore practical rather than explicitly paranormal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/a-historia-da-ufologia-oficial-no-uruguai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[Revista UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span></span></span>

Unlike many civilian UFO organisations, CRIDOVNI operates within the framework of the Air Force's responsibility for monitoring Uruguayan airspace. That gives investigators access to information unavailable to ordinary enthusiasts, including:

* civil and military flight records;
* radar information where available;
* meteorological observations;
* astronomical data;
* satellite and space-debris tracking;
* witness interviews conducted using standard procedures.

</div>

The commission has also collaborated with civilian specialists, including astronomers, meteorologists and experienced civilian investigators, recognising that unusual aerial reports often require expertise from several disciplines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/a-historia-da-ufologia-oficial-no-uruguai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[Revista UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span></span></span>

## How official UFO reports are checked

One reason Uruguay's files attract attention is that CRIDOVNI has publicly described much of its investigative process.

Rather than asking whether a witness has seen an alien craft, investigators begin with more ordinary questions. They assess whether the report could involve deliberate fraud, misunderstanding by witnesses, conventional aircraft, atmospheric or astronomical phenomena, optical illusions or psychological factors. Only after these possibilities have been considered does a case remain classified as unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/a-historia-da-ufologia-oficial-no-uruguai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[Revista UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span></span></span>

The investigation may include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* interviewing all available witnesses separately;
* comparing accounts with known aircraft movements;
* checking weather conditions and atmospheric phenomena;
* identifying planets, bright stars, satellites or meteor activity;
* reviewing photographs or video for signs of misidentification or artefacts;
* examining any claimed physical traces where appropriate.

</div>

This approach resembles accident investigation more than paranormal research. The goal is to identify the observed phenomenon whenever possible, not to defend a particular explanation.

## What the files actually show

One of the most frequently misunderstood aspects of Uruguay's UFO archive is its statistical record.

According to figures repeatedly published by CRIDOVNI officials, the commission has investigated well over 1,500and by some accounts more than 2,000reports since its creation. A substantial proportion could not be analysed because the available information was too incomplete. Of the cases that could be properly investigated, the overwhelming majority were eventually attributed to conventional causes such as aircraft, astronomical objects, meteorological events, birds, insects close to camera lenses, or photographic artefacts. Only a small residual percentage remained officially categorised as "non-conventional" or unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/a-historia-da-ufologia-oficial-no-uruguai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[Revista UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span></span></span>

That statistical pattern is important.

An unresolved case is simply one for which investigators cannot confidently identify the object using the available evidence. It is not equivalent to a confirmed extraterrestrial vehicle.

This distinction is sometimes lost in popular retellings. A military file labelled "unidentified" means precisely that: investigators could not identify it. It does not establish what the object actually was.

## The unresolved residue and its limits

The small proportion of unresolved reports has naturally attracted the greatest attention from UFO researchers.

CRIDOVNI officials have described recurring features reported in some difficult cases, including unusual manoeuvres, rapid acceleration, changing colours and occasional radar detections. Some investigations have also recorded claims of ground traces or electromagnetic effects. These observations are presented as witness reports or investigative findings rather than proof of extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/a-historia-da-ufologia-oficial-no-uruguai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[Revista UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span></span></span>

Several limitations prevent stronger conclusions.

First, many unusual aerial events are observed only briefly and under poor viewing conditions. Night-time observations make judging distance, size and speed extremely difficult.

Second, not every report contains multiple independent witnesses or supporting radar information. A dramatic testimony may still lack enough evidence for firm identification.

Third, even apparently unusual radar returns or physical traces can have more than one possible explanation. Without repeatable measurements or recoverable material, investigators cannot conclusively determine what produced them.

For these reasons, CRIDOVNI has consistently avoided declaring that unresolved cases prove extraterrestrial visitation. Instead, it treats them as open investigations that may eventually receive conventional explanations if new evidence becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/a-historia-da-ufologia-oficial-no-uruguai/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[Revista UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Revista UFOA histria da Ufologia Oficial no Uruguai Revista UFO</span></span></span>

## Transparency without certainty

One feature that distinguishes Uruguay from many countries is its comparatively open discussion of official UFO investigations.

Senior CRIDOVNI representatives have regularly given interviews explaining the commission's procedures, statistics and general findings. Public access requests have also demonstrated that the commission remains an active point of contact for reports, even if not every document is immediately released.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gub.uy/unidad-acceso-informacion-publica/institucional/normativa/resolucion-n-16020-sobre-entrega-informacion-informacion-reservada" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gub.uy">[Gub.uy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gub.uy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">July 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 10, 2020</span></span></span>

This transparency has sometimes been misunderstood as confirmation that governments possess secret knowledge about alien visitors. In reality, openness mainly demonstrates that Uruguay considers unidentified aerial reports worthy of systematic documentation rather than ridicule.

That approach has practical value regardless of whether any report ultimately proves extraordinary. Aviation authorities benefit from investigating anything that pilots or air-traffic personnel cannot immediately identify.

## So what do Uruguay's UFO files really prove?

The strongest conclusions supported by the evidence are more modestand arguably more interestingthan many sensational headlines suggest.

Uruguay's Air Force files show that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* governments can investigate UFO reports seriously without assuming paranormal explanations;
* most reports eventually receive ordinary explanations after systematic analysis;
* a small number of cases remain unidentified because available evidence is incomplete or inconclusive;
* maintaining official archives creates a valuable historical record of unusual aerial observations.

</div>

They do **not** prove that extraterrestrial spacecraft have visited Uruguay, nor do they demonstrate a government cover-up or secret confirmation of alien technology.

For students of Forteana, the enduring fascination of Uruguay's archive lies precisely in this balance. The files preserve genuine mysteries, but they also document the patient, often unspectacular work of trying to separate misidentification, folklore, atmospheric phenomena and the occasional stubbornly unexplained report. That combination of curiosity and caution is what makes Uruguay's official UFO record one of the most distinctive in Latin America.
